## Combining Vectors :

- [[Key Terms#Scalar|Scalars]] only have ([[Key Terms#Vector in a plane & Magnitude|Magnitude]]), and no direction.
- If you <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">multiply a vector by a scalar</mark> the <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">vectors magnitude changes</mark>. This is called 
<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">(Scalar multiplication)</mark>
- if you multiply a [[Key Terms#Vector|vector]] and a scalar <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">the only thing that changes</mark> is the <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">magnitude</mark> and the direction does not change.
## Rule of scalar multiplication

$\vec{KV}$

→   If $k > 0$ :

- The new vector points is the same direction as $\vec{V}$ 
	→ Its magnitude  is $|k|$ times longer

→ If $k<0$ :

- The new vector points in the opposite direction.

→ If $k = 0$:

- The result is the [[Key Terms#Zero vector|zero vector]] (Just a point at the origin).
